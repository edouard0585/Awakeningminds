Why is it so hard to fall asleep when you try?

The evening paradox

You turn off the light — and that's exactly when the mind turns on: the day replays, tomorrow's list writes itself, one sentence from noon loops endlessly. The harder you try to sleep, the further it recedes — because trying is a waking state.

A meditation to fall asleep takes the opposite road: it doesn't chase sleep, it prepares the conditions where sleep arrives on its own. You give attention one simple object — the breath, the body's sensations, a calm voice — and the mind, no longer alone on stage, lowers the curtain.

What's the best position for a sleep meditation?

The one time falling asleep counts as success

Lying down — savasana — is the posture of choice at night. Everywhere else in meditation, falling asleep is an obstacle; here, it's the triumphant exit. To settle in:

  • On your back, arms alongside the body, palms up.
  • A thin pillow under the head; another under the knees if the lower back pulls.
  • In bed, under the covers: the one context where meditating in bed is recommended — you won't have to move when sleep arrives.

How do you do 4-7-8 breathing for sleep?

A long exhale is the body's natural brake

Of all breathing techniques, 4-7-8 is the sleep one: inhale for 4, hold for 7, exhale slowly for 8. The long exhale does the work — it slows the heart rate and tells the body the night can begin.

  1. First exhale completely, through the mouth.
  2. Inhale through the nose for a count of 4.
  3. Hold for a count of 7 — without tensing the shoulders.
  4. Exhale slowly through the mouth for a count of 8, like one long sigh.
  5. Repeat 4 to 8 cycles, then let the breath return to normal.
⚠️ CautionBreath retention is not advised during pregnancy or with heart or breathing difficulties: replace it with a simple exhale twice as long as the inhale — the calming effect is the same.

How does a guided sleep meditation work?

A voice that does the thinking for you

A guided sleep meditation does exactly what the mind refuses to do alone at night: it gently occupies attention. The voice travels the body region by region (the evening body scan), offers calm imagery, slows its own sentences, and leaves longer and longer silences. There is nothing to achieve: just follow, and let yourself be left behind.

What makes a good sleep meditation?

  • Length: 10 to 20 minutes is enough — sleep often arrives before the end.
  • Voice: pick the one that lulls you, at a slow speed.
  • The ending: a session that closes in silence — no end music, no chime.

How do you fall back asleep at 3 a.m.?

When the night breaks in the middle

Waking during the night is normal — falling back asleep is what can be trained. Here's how to fall back asleep, in three rules:

  • No screen, no clock. Checking the time starts the mental math ("3 h 40 left…") — exactly what to avoid.
  • Return to the body, not the thoughts. A slow body scan from the feet up, or a few long-exhale cycles.
  • If restlessness wins: a gentle guided meditation in earphones beats forty minutes of struggling in the dark. “Drifting into Sleep” is made for this: it ends with no wake-up cue.
