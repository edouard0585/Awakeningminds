Do five minutes actually count?

What duration does — and doesn't do

Yes — and not as a consolation prize. A 5-minute meditation is not a 20-minute meditation shrunk down: it's a different tool with its own job. Twenty minutes dig deep; five minutes interrupt — they break the momentum of a runaway day, put the breath back at the center, and make the next decision clearer.

Above all: five minutes kept every day build the habit that twenty minutes "when I find the time" never will. Regularity beats duration — the most solid rule in all of learning to meditate.

Three short formats that work

Each has its use

Coherent breathing — measurable calm

Six breaths per minute for five minutes: inhale 5 seconds, exhale 5 seconds, without forcing. The simplest guided breathing exercise there is — and one of the best documented.

The express body scan — coming back to the body

One minute per floor: feet and legs, hips and belly, chest and breath, shoulders and hands, face and head. Nothing to change — just to feel. Ideal after a tense meeting or before walking in your front door: the day stays outside.

5-4-3-2-1 grounding — when everything spins

Five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch, two you can smell, one long exhale. Attention leaves the mental movie and returns to the room. Discreet enough for a train, a queue, a hallway.

Where to place those five minutes

The hinges of the day

A short pause is strongest at transitions — the moments you change roles:

  • Before starting — coffee still warm, before the first screen.
  • Between blocks — after a hard meeting, before an important call.
  • The doorstep — in the parked car or before opening the door: work stays outside.
  • Before a decision — five calm minutes often beat an hour of hesitation.
