Mindfulness, in one sentence

What the word actually covers

Mindfulness is paying attention to the present moment, on purpose, without judging what you find there. Three words, three demands: the moment (not the memory, not the scenario), the intention (you choose to be there), the absence of judgement (you welcome what is, discomfort included).

So it isn't a state of permanent calm, nor a way to "empty the mind". It is a way of paying attention — one that is learned, lost, and found again, dozens of times per session.

"Mindfulness is the awareness that arises from paying attention, on purpose, in the present moment, non-judgmentally." — Jon Kabat-Zinn
2

What it is not

Four stubborn misunderstandings

It isn't emptying the mind

A mind that produces thoughts is a mind that works. You aren't trying to stop them — you notice them passing, and you come back.

It isn't a religion

The techniques come from contemplative traditions, but the practice today is entirely secular: everyone brings the meaning they want, or none at all.

It isn't forced positivity

Non-judgemental means exactly that. A hard day stays hard — you meet it as it is, which is very different from convincing yourself it's fine.

It isn't only for calm people

Busy minds are precisely the ones the practice serves most. "I think too much to meditate" is like saying "I'm too out of breath to run".

3

The core move: notice and return

The one exercise, repeated

The whole practice fits into one endlessly repeating cycle: attention rests somewhere — the breath, usually —, it wanders off, you notice that it wandered, you bring it back. That third beat, the noticing, is the key moment: it alone is what trains attention.

Hence the most useful reversal for a beginner: a mind that wanders often isn't failing often — it's training often. Fifty returns in one session are fifty repetitions.

4

Practising, formally and informally

On the cushion, and in real life

Formal practice

Dedicated time, seated: 10 minutes of attention on the breath, or a guided mindfulness meditation — the body scan is its most accessible form. This is where the move gets installed.

Informal practice

The same move, slipped into an ordinary activity: feeling the water while washing up, truly listening to someone, walking while noticing your footfalls. Three conscious breaths before opening a door are enough — and that's where the practice really changes a day.
