Meditating in daily life

Adapting practice to your life situations

💼 At work

  • Transitions: 3 conscious breaths between two tasks or before each meeting — the brain closes one file before opening another, instead of stacking.
  • The irritating email: never answer on the first wave. STOP (1 min), then answer — or better, RAIN first if the wave is big.
  • Meditative single-tasking: pick a 25-minute task and do it as a meditation: a single window, notifications off, and return to the task at each distraction, as you return to the breath.
  • The screen break: every hour, 20 seconds of looking into the distance + 3 abdominal breaths. Your eyes and vagus nerve will thank you.

📚 Studies et exams

  • Before a study session: 5 minutes of mindful breathing — stabilized attention upstream pays off every minute after.
  • Stage fright: the day before, visualization (the “safe place,” then the exam scene unfolding calmly); just before the test, box breathing 4-4-4-4 or physiological sigh.
  • Going blank: during the test, if everything freezes: put down the pen, express 5-4-3-2-1 (3 things seen, 2 heard, 1 touched), resume. 30 seconds that save 30 minutes.

🏃 Sport et body

  • Before effort: a few abdominal breaths + a lightning scan (contact points, shoulders, jaw) to start relaxed.
  • During: endurance is a ready-made moving meditation — the rhythm of steps or pedal strokes as anchor, exactly as in walking meditation.
  • After: body scan in recovery accelerates relaxation; the still-warm body makes sensations very readable — ideal for progressing in body awareness.
  • Competition: visualization of the successful move + a fixed breathing routine before each performance (the consistency of the ritual matters as much as the technique).

👨‍👩‍👧 With family — meditating with children

Children meditate willingly if it's short, concrete and playful. Golden rule: about one minute per year of age, and always offer, never impose.

  • The breathing plush (3-6 years): lying down, a plush on the belly — make it rise and fall gently with the breath. It's abdominal breathing, disguised as a game.
  • Inner weather (from 4 years): “what's the weather like inside?” Sun, clouds, storm? You observe the weather, you don't punish it — first lesson in emotional recognition.
  • The listening minute (from 5 years): eyes closed, raise your hand when you can no longer hear the bell (or bowl). Then: “how many different sounds did you hear?”
  • For teens: offer the micro-practices useful for THEIR concerns (stage fright, sleep, class focus) rather than “meditation” in general — use creates buy-in.
💡 The best leverA parent who visibly (and serenely) practices does more to inspire meditation than all the exercises in the world.

🚇 Transport, waiting et screens

  • Metro, bus, train: perfect sound meditation (the din becomes the support) or anchor on the body's contact points and swayings.
  • Queues: rename them “free breaks”: feet on floor, breath, shoulders. Impatience itself is an excellent object of observation.
  • Attentional hygiene: every reflex phone unlock is anti-meditation training. A simple counterweight: one conscious breath before unlocking. You'll see how many times your hand moves on its own — that's already mindfulness.
