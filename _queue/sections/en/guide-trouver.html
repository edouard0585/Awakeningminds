Who is my spirit guide?

No one can tell you from the outside

Your spirit guide is the presence that keeps showing up for you, with kindness. It may come in meditation, in dreams or in daily life. No reading, quiz or stranger can name it for you. You find out by meeting it yourself, over time.

Traditions give spirit guides many faces. In the app, Your Guides offers eight types: Sage, Spirit Animal, Ancestor, Angel, Shadow, Archetype, Extraterrestrial and Other. Your guide might be a wise elder, an animal or a grandmother you never met. It might also be a figure of light, or simply a calm voice.

You may see guides as beings in their own right, or as wise parts of your own mind. Either way, the approach is the same. Make room, ask with an open heart and pay attention to what comes back.

How do you prepare to meet your spirit guide?

Time, place and a sincere intention

Choose a quiet moment of about 20 minutes, a place where no one will disturb you and one simple intention. Preparation matters more than technique.

  • Pick your moment. Rested and calm, not right after an argument or a heavy meal.
  • Set up your space. Phone on silent, soft light, a notebook within reach.
  • Choose your intention. For example: “I would like to meet a guide who wishes me well.”
  • Drop your expectations. You may meet a figure, a feeling or nothing at all, and all three are fine.

How do you find your spirit guide in meditation?

A seven-step method

Relax, travel to an inner place, invite a guide and welcome whatever comes, then give thanks and return. Here is the method, step by step.

  1. Ground and relax. Sit or lie down. Breathe out slowly a few times, and feel the weight of your body.
  2. Follow a path. Picture a path, a staircase or a bridge that leads to a peaceful place: a clearing, a garden, a shore.
  3. Settle into your meeting place. Notice the light, the sounds and the ground under your feet. Take your time.
  4. Invite your guide. Silently repeat your intention, then wait. Do not try to create anyone.
  5. Notice who comes. A person, an animal, a light, a presence behind you. Greet it, and ask its name if you wish.
  6. Ask one simple question. For example: “What would help me most this week?” Listen without forcing an answer.
  7. Give thanks and return. Thank your guide, walk back the way you came, open your eyes and write everything down.

How do you know it is really your guide?

Telling a guide from wishful thinking

A real guide, however you understand it, tends to come back and to feel calm and kind. It says things that help, even when you would rather not hear them.

  • It returns. The same figure, or the same feeling, shows up across several meditations or dreams.
  • It feels peaceful. Its presence calms you, even when its message is hard.
  • It surprises you. It rarely says exactly what you hoped for. Wishful thinking usually does.
  • It respects your freedom. It suggests, and never commands.
  • Its advice holds up. What it brings still makes sense in daily life, days later.

Swiss psychologist Carl Jung explored this question through what he called active imagination. He let inner figures speak while staying awake and critical. His approach is still useful today: welcome what comes, then judge it by its effects on your life.

What if no spirit guide appears?

Silence is part of the path

This is very common, and it does not mean you have no guide. Often you are trying too hard, or the guide comes in another form.

  • Some people never see a figure, but feel warmth, a presence or a sudden calm.
  • Others meet their guide later, in a dream or during a walk in nature.
  • Effort blocks the process. Try again another day, more relaxed and with fewer expectations.
  • Short, regular sessions work better than one long attempt. Once or twice a week is plenty.

On such days, The Ten Steps to the Temple of Light, 21 minutes, can help. You walk down ten steps toward water that grows still as the mind settles.

How do you learn your spirit guide's name?

And what if it never comes

Ask once, in meditation, and accept the first word, sound or image that comes. If nothing comes, you can choose a name yourself.

Names often arrive in odd ways: a single syllable, a name from another language, a word you only understand later. Some come in dreams, days after you asked. Write them down, even if they seem strange.

Many people never receive a name, and their guide is no less present. You can call it by what it is, such as the gardener or the gray owl. Or give it a name that feels right. In Your Guides, each guide has its own page for its name, type, appearance and messages. A log records its Reappearances.

Can your spirit guide be an animal?

Power animals and animal guides

Yes. Animal guides are among the oldest and most widespread. In shamanic traditions, a power animal protects the person and teaches through its nature. Think of the patience of the heron, or the courage of the bear.

An animal guide often makes itself known by repetition. It returns in your meditations, crosses your path in daily life or visits your dreams. You may also have felt drawn to one animal since childhood.

The phrase spirit animal is closely linked to Indigenous traditions. Out of respect, many people now prefer animal guide or power animal. What should you avoid when looking for a spirit guide?

Six warning signs

Avoid anything that frightens you, flatters you, isolates you or costs a lot. A trustworthy guide needs none of these.

  • An inner figure that threatens you or demands obedience.
  • Messages saying that you are chosen or superior, or that no one else can understand you.
  • Advice to cut ties with the people close to you.
  • Paid readings that pressure you to buy more to “unlock” your guide.
  • Substances or extreme fasting to force contact.
  • Big life decisions made on the strength of a single message, with no time to think.

If a meeting goes wrong, end the meditation, ground yourself and come back another day.

⚠️ When to ask for helpIf voices or presences appear outside meditation, or if these practices leave you shaken, take a break. Then talk to a professional.
