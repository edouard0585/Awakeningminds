Chakras et the energy body

The inner map used by energy meditations

What is a chakra?

Traditional definition: in yoga and tantra, the chakras (“wheels” in Sanskrit) are energy centers stacked along the spine, each associated with a body area, a color, and a dimension of life (safety, emotions, will, love, expression, intuition, connection). The “energy body” or “light body” is the whole of this subtle network.

Psychological lens: no sensor has ever measured a chakra — but the map works remarkably as a language of interoception: the throat that tightens when you dare not speak, the plexus that knots under stress, the chest that warms in affection are universal experiences. Meditating on the chakras is systematically visiting these crossroad zones where the body somatizes, with a color and an intention to guide attention. Both lenses lead to the same exercises.

How to meditate with the chakras — the 3 signature practices

① The traversal (reset) — guided balancing

Move up from the root to the crown: at each center, breathe “into” it, visualize its color as a soft light, and let an affirmation resonate (“I am safe,” “I allow myself to feel,” “I express my truth”…). About 2 minutes per center. Perfect at night, or when you feel “out of tune” without knowing why.

② The dialogue — one center at a time

  1. Choose the center that “calls”: the body zone that is most tense or most dulled today.
  2. Breathe into this zone for 10 breaths, hand placed on it if possible.
  3. Give it voice: “if this zone could speak, what would it say?” Welcome words, images, memories — it's your interoception putting itself into story.
  4. Ask what it needs, and offer it through visualization: space, warmth, rest, color.
  5. Finish by reunifying: the whole body breathing as one.

③ Third eye activation — entry into inner vision

The signature practice of inner journeys: attention placed between the eyebrows, forehead relaxed, eyes at ease. You observe what appears behind the eyelids (darkness, glimmers, forms) without forcing, and let these impressions become a space (see the Symbols et inner worlds chapter). It's the bridge between classical concentration and imaginal exploration.

⚠️ Safety markersStrong sensations (warmth, tingling, pressure at the forehead) are frequent and benign — they follow attention as salivation follows the idea of lemon. If an energy practice generates anxiety, persistent dizziness or unusual restlessness: return to the anchor (root, feet, breath), shorten, and space out sessions. Always end a chakra practice “from below”: a few breaths at the feet and on the ground.
