Chakras and the energy body

The inner map used by energy meditations

What is a chakra?

Traditional definition: in yoga and tantra, the chakras (“wheels” in Sanskrit) are energy centers stacked along the spine, each associated with a body area, a color, and a dimension of life (safety, emotions, will, love, expression, intuition, connection). The “energy body” or “light body” is the whole of this subtle network.

Psychological lens: no sensor has ever measured a chakra — but the map works remarkably well as a language of interoception, your sense of what is happening inside your body: the throat that tightens when you dare not speak, the solar plexus that knots under stress, the chest that warms with affection are universal experiences. Meditating on the chakras means visiting these crossroads one by one, the places where emotions show up in the body, with a color and an intention to guide your attention. Both lenses lead to the same exercises.

What are the 7 chakras and where are they located?

The chakra locations follow the spine, from its base to the top of the head. Here is the chakra order used in most meditations, from bottom to top:

1 · Root (Muladhara) — base of the spine · red · safety and grounding.

2 · Sacral (Svadhisthana) — lower belly, below the navel · orange · emotions, pleasure and creativity.

3 · Solar plexus (Manipura) — upper belly, between the navel and the breastbone · yellow · willpower and confidence.

4 · Heart (Anahata) — center of the chest · green · love and compassion.

5 · Throat (Vishuddha) — throat and neck · blue · voice and expression.

6 · Third eye (Ajna) — between the eyebrows · indigo · intuition and inner vision.

7 · Crown (Sahasrara) — top of the head · violet · connection and meaning.

These rainbow colors are a modern convention, now used in most guided meditations. Older Indian texts give the centers other colors and symbols, and some count more or fewer of them.

What are the main chakra meditation techniques?

Three practices cover the essentials: the full journey from root to crown, a dialogue with a single center, and third eye activation. If you're new to chakras, start with the first.

① How do you balance your chakras?

Move up from the root to the crown: at each center, breathe “into” it, visualize its color as a soft light, and let an affirmation resonate (“I am safe,” “I allow myself to feel,” “I express my truth”…). About 2 minutes per center, so about 15 minutes in all. Perfect at night, or when you feel “out of tune” without knowing why.

② How do you work with a single center?

  1. Choose the center that “calls”: the body zone that is most tense or most numb today.
  2. Breathe into this zone for 10 breaths, hand placed on it if possible.
  3. Give it voice: “if this zone could speak, what would it say?” Welcome words, images, memories — it's your inner sense of the body telling its story.
  4. Ask what it needs, and offer it through visualization: space, warmth, rest, color.
  5. Finish by reunifying: the whole body breathing as one.

③ How do you activate your third eye?

Place your attention between your eyebrows, forehead relaxed, eyes at ease: it's the signature practice of inner journeys. You observe what appears behind the eyelids (darkness, glimmers, forms) without forcing, and let these impressions become a space (see the Symbols and inner worlds chapter). It's the bridge between classical concentration and imaginal exploration.

⚠️ Safety notesStrong sensations (warmth, tingling, pressure at the forehead) are frequent and benign — they follow attention as salivation follows the idea of lemon. If an energy practice generates anxiety, persistent dizziness or unusual restlessness: return to the anchor (root, feet, breath), shorten, and space out sessions. Always end a chakra practice “from below”: a few breaths at the feet and on the ground.

How can you use this body map during the day?

As a one-minute body check. Close your eyes and scan from the base of your spine to the top of your head: where do you feel tension right now — a tight throat, a knotted stomach, a heavy chest? Send three slow breaths to that place, then come back down to your feet. Done a few times a day, this habit helps you notice stress early, while it's still easy to release.
