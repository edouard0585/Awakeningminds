Meditation course for beginners: how do you learn to meditate in 4 weeks?

From the very first breath to autonomy

One new skill each week, and a few minutes a day is enough: posture and breathing, then attention, then the body, then mindfulness and new techniques. Each week consolidates the previous one. If you miss a day, simply resume the next — regularity rebuilds itself, it doesn't get lost.

Here is the full meditation schedule for beginners, week by week:

WeekGoalDaily practice
Week 1
Lay the foundations		 Posture + abdominal breathing 5 min/day — Set up posture (2 min), then hand on belly: 10 abdominal breaths, and free observation of the breath. Days 6–7: try 5-5 coherent breathing with the app's interactive breathing guide.
Week 2
Anchor attention		 Mindful breathing + attention cycle 8 min/day — Mindful breathing with counting 1 to 10. Train “noticing and returning”: each return counts. Every other day, finish with 2 min of sounds.
Week 3
Explore the body		 Body scan + walking 10 min/day — Alternate: body scan (odd days), walking meditation (even days). Add a daily anchor: 3 conscious breaths at a chosen trigger-gesture.
Week 4
Open up		 Mindfulness + discovery 12–15 min/day — Full mindfulness session (breath → sounds → sensations → open presence). Try 2 new techniques (Metta, mantra, visualization…) to see which ones suit you.
💡 What should you do after the 4 weeks?You're autonomous: keep a daily base of 10–15 minutes with your favorite technique, and pick other techniques according to the day's needs. Next goal: a 20-minute session, once a week. To keep the habit going after that, read our guide to daily meditation.

Do you need meditation classes for beginners, or can you learn at home?

You can learn at home. A class gives you a teacher and a fixed appointment, and that helps some people stay on track. But the basics are simple enough to learn alone, as long as you have a clear structure.
