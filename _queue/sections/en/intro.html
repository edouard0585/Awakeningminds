What meditation is — and what it isn't

What is meditation?

To meditate is to train your attention. Like a muscle, attention gets stronger through repetition: you choose an anchor point (often the breath), you notice when the mind wanders, and you gently bring it back. That's all. This simple gesture, repeated hundreds of times, progressively transforms your relationship with your thoughts and emotions.

“You can't stop the waves, but you can learn to surf.” — Jon Kabat-Zinn

✅ Meditation is…

  • Training of attention, accessible to everyone
  • Observing what happens, without judging
  • Coming back again and again to your anchor
  • A secular practice, backed by research

❌ It is not…

  • “Emptying the mind” or stopping thoughts
  • Mandatory relaxation (sometimes you get restless, that's normal)
  • Reserved for “zen” or flexible people
  • A religion, nor a performance to succeed at

Why meditate? The benefits

🧠 Mental

Better concentration, distance from rumination, clarity in decisions, stronger working memory.

💗 Emotional

Less stress and anxiety, better emotional regulation, more patience and kindness toward yourself.

🌙 Physical

Deeper sleep, calmer blood pressure, muscle relaxation, fuller everyday breathing.

🔬 Good to knowEffects appear gradually: studies show measurable changes (stress, attention) after about 8 weeks of regular practice — even at 10 minutes a day. Regularity matters more than duration.

The 5 misconceptions that block beginners

“I can't do it, I'm thinking all the time”
Thinking is normal: the brain produces thoughts the way the heart beats. The goal is not to suppress them, but to notice that you've drifted and come back. Each return IS meditation — it's a successful repetition, not a failure.
“You have to meditate for a long time for it to help”
False. 5 minutes a day, every day, is better than one hour on Sunday. Start short, duration will come naturally.
“You have to sit in lotus”
No posture is required. A chair works perfectly. The only rule: a back that stays upright without tension (see the Posture section).
“Meditating means relaxing”
Relaxation is a frequent effect, not the goal. Some sessions are restless or uncomfortable — they train attention just as much, sometimes more.
“I don't have time”
Meditation fits everywhere: 3 conscious breaths before a meeting, 5 minutes on waking, a mindful walk. The Tips section shows how to anchor it in an existing routine.
