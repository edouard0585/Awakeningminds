What should you know before you start meditating?

What meditation is — and what it isn't

What is meditation?

To meditate is to train your attention. Like a muscle, attention gets stronger through repetition: you choose an anchor point (often the breath), you notice when the mind wanders, and you gently bring it back. That's all. This simple gesture, repeated hundreds of times, progressively transforms your relationship with your thoughts and emotions.

“You can't stop the waves, but you can learn to surf.” — Jon Kabat-Zinn

✅ Meditation is…

  • Training of attention, accessible to everyone
  • Observing what happens, without judging
  • Coming back again and again to your anchor
  • A secular practice, backed by research

❌ It is not…

  • “Emptying the mind” or stopping thoughts
  • Mandatory relaxation (sometimes you get restless, that's normal)
  • Reserved for “zen” or flexible people
  • A religion, nor a performance to succeed at

What are the benefits of meditation?

🧠 Mental

Better concentration, distance from rumination, clarity in decisions, stronger working memory.

💗 Emotional

Less stress and anxiety, better emotional regulation, more patience and kindness toward yourself.

🌙 Physical

Deeper sleep, a calmer body, muscle relaxation, fuller everyday breathing.

🔬 Good to knowEffects appear gradually: studies show measurable changes (stress, attention) after about 8 weeks of regular practice — even at 10 minutes a day. Regularity matters more than duration.

Which myths keep beginners from meditating?

“I can't do it, I'm thinking all the time”
Thinking is normal: the brain produces thoughts the way the heart beats. The goal is not to suppress them, but to notice that you've drifted and come back. Each return IS meditation — it's a successful repetition, not a failure.
“You have to meditate for a long time for it to help”
False. 5 minutes a day, every day, is better than one hour on Sunday. Start short, duration will come naturally.
“You have to sit in lotus”
No posture is required. A chair works perfectly. The only rule: a back that stays upright without tension (see the posture guide).
“Meditating means relaxing”
Relaxation is a frequent effect, not the goal. Some sessions are restless or uncomfortable — they train attention just as much, sometimes more.
“I don't have time”
Meditation fits everywhere: 3 conscious breaths before a meeting, 5 minutes on waking, a mindful walk. To make it stick, attach it to a habit you already have, like your morning coffee.

How to meditate in your first week

A concrete path: first session, posture, breath, rhythm, mistakes

Your first session, in five steps

  1. Pick a fixed time, right after waking up or after work. Five minutes is plenty to start: that is the length of “Your First Silent Meditation”.
  2. Set your posture: sit on a chair, feet flat, back straight without stiffness, hands resting on your thighs. Close your eyes, or rest your gaze on the floor about three feet ahead.
  3. Rest your attention on the breath, without changing it: feel the air come in and go out, at the nostrils or in the belly.
  4. When the mind wanders, come back: notice the thought, label it with one word (“plan”, “memory”), then gently return to the breath. That return is the exercise.
  5. Close gently: three fuller breaths, then open your eyes and jot down one line about how you feel.

How often should you meditate?

Five minutes every day beats an hour on Sunday. In week one, aim for six days out of seven; in week two, move up to 10 or 15 minutes if you feel like it. An evening session lying down counts too.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Trying to empty your mind: thoughts come back, that is normal. Meditating means watching them pass.
  • Grading every session: a restless session trains attention as much as a calm one.
  • Starting too long: 30 minutes on day one is discouraging; 5 to 10 minutes is sustainable.
  • Switching techniques daily: keep the same anchor, the breath, for at least a week.
  • Slumping or bracing: neither collapsed nor rigid, just a straight back and soft shoulders.
