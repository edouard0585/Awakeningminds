Symbols, archetypes et inner worlds

The language of guided journeys — and how to interpret it

Why inner journeys speak in symbols

As soon as the analytical mind steps back (trance, deep visualization), the psyche expresses itself as in dreams: in images, in places, in characters, in objects. A key received, a closed door, an animal that guides — these are not decorations: it is your inner language. Learning to read it is THE skill of exploration meditations.

How to interpret a symbol — the rule of 3 questions

  1. What does it represent for ME? Your personal association takes precedence over any symbol dictionary: a dog can be comfort for one, threat for another.
  2. What did I feel in its presence? The emotion accompanying the symbol is half the message: the same tower seen in peace or in anxiety doesn't say the same thing.
  3. What does this echo in my current life? The symbol almost always comments on the present: a decision, a relationship, a blockage of today.
💡 The notebook reflexWrite down every striking symbol (object received, guide, place) in your journal after the session. Reread over weeks, they draw astonishing red threads — and some return from session to session, like recurring characters in your inner story.

The great symbolic places of advanced explorations

📚 The inner library et the “akashic records”

Traditional definition: the akashic records would be the “universal memory” where everything is recorded, accessible in altered states. Psychological lens: the infinite library is one of the most beautiful symbolic interfaces with your own memory and implicit knowledge — you “consult a book” as you ask a question of your depths, and the answer rises in images. Use: ask ONE clear question, let a book come to you, open it without forcing, welcome what appears (words, scenes, symbols). The value of the content is judged as always: by the next-day test.

🏛 The memory palace

Ancient memorization technique (the “loci”) turned meditative journey: build a stable, detailed inner place that you visit regularly. Each room can host a memory, a resource, a symbolic object received in meditation. The more you visit it, the more real it becomes — it's the older sibling of the “safe place,” and the cousin of the inner sanctuary.

🔷 Sacred geometry

Meditating on pure forms (circle, spiral, mandala, flower of life): symmetry naturally captures attention (like a mandala you color), inducing calm and absorption. No mysterious properties demonstrated — but a truly effective concentration support, and a soothing visual language every culture has explored.

🌅 The future self et manifestation

Dialogue with “yourself in 5 years” or visualize an achieved goal. What psychology validates: visualization orients attention and motivation (you notice opportunities aligned with the image), and process imagery (seeing yourself DOING the steps) truly improves performance. What it doesn't validate: the idea that visualization “attracts” the object by itself. The formula that works: clear vision + embodied feeling + first concrete step within 24 h.
