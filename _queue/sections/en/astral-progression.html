Astral progression, step by step

The full ladder: from trance to separation techniques

Why a ladder? Beginner mistake #1

Most beginners fail at astral travel for a simple reason: they attempt the full exit without having built the rungs below. The ability to “exit” rests on three stacked skills: holding a deep trance with body asleep / mind awake, vividly feeling an “imagined body” distinct from the physical body, and only then, chaining a separation technique. Each rung is trained separately — that's exactly how the Trance, Micro-detachments and Astral journeys menus are organized.

Rung 1 — Preparatory trance techniques

Goal: reach at will the “body asleep / mind awake” state. The tools, from simplest to finest:

  • Deep descent: very long, progressive relaxation (extended yoga nidra type) to total heaviness — the long version of chapter 10's trance.
  • Fractional induction (inherited from Ericksonian hypnosis): descend, come back halfway, descend deeper — each cycle deepens the trance, as you drive a stake in with small taps.
  • Progressive visualization: build a scene of ever-increasing detail to absorb all the mind into imagery.
  • The mental screen: observe the dark space behind the eyelids until hypnagogic images appear on their own — then watch them without grabbing.
  • The subtle senses: in trance, explore one by one the “imagined senses” (touch an imaginary texture, hear an inner sound) — the direct training of the subtle body.
💡 Passage criterionYou're ready for rung 2 when you regularly reach: complete heaviness, spontaneous images behind the eyes, and time distortion — without falling asleep.

Rung 2 — Micro-detachments (the key discovery)

The principle: rather than trying to “exit” in one block — which freezes most beginners — you learn to detach only one part of the imagined body at a time. In trance, physical body still, you move a “phantom limb”: it's a kinesthetic imagery sensation, surprisingly vivid, that trains like a muscle.

  • The phantom hand: without moving a muscle, “lift” your imagined hand. Feel it lift off, float, touch an imaginary object. The founding exercise.
  • The ceiling magnet: feel a gentle pull drawing the imagined chest upward.
  • The fishing line: a line gently pulls a point of the subtle body (forehead, sternum) forward.
  • The slide: the imagined body slides a few centimeters toward the feet, as on a very soft slide.
  • The phantom sit-up: the imagined torso sits up while the physical body stays lying down.
  • The tip / fall: let yourself “sink” through the mattress, or tip backward.

One session = ONE micro-gesture, repeated with patience. The day a phantom limb becomes as clear as a real one, the full techniques become almost easy.

Vibrations: the signpost

At the threshold of separation, vibrations often occur — buzzing, gentle electric waves, inner acceleration. This is the hallmark of the transition state (well documented in accounts, from Monroe to contemporary practitioners). Golden rule: don't react. Neither fear nor excitement — both dissolve the state. Let the vibrations settle, amplify on their own, and then trigger your technique, calmly.

After the exit: stabilize then explore

  1. The first meters: aim for nothing grand — move a meter away, observe the room, come back. Like a diver first learning to breathe underwater.
  2. Moving: intention replaces the legs — think of the destination, glide there.
  3. Choose destinations, then keep a map of your explorations in the journal: recurring places become landmarks, your inner geography takes shape.
  4. Return: think of your body, your hand, want to move a finger — the return is instantaneous. Then full grounding and immediate notes.
⚠️ Reminders from chapter 10Occasional (1–2 attempts/week), never with a sleep debt, sleep paralysis = normal and passing, and “objective” verifications to consider with kindly critical thinking: the value of these journeys is in the experience and what it teaches you, not in a proof to be established.
