How do you learn astral projection, step by step?

The full ladder: from trance to separation techniques

How to astral project: why do most beginners fail?

Most beginners fail at astral travel for a simple reason: they attempt the full exit without having built the rungs below. The ability to “exit” rests on three stacked skills: holding a deep trance with body asleep / mind awake, vividly feeling an “imagined body” distinct from the physical body, and only then, moving on to a separation technique. Each rung is trained separately — that's exactly how the Trance, Micro-detachments and Astral journeys menus are organized. So how to astral project for beginners comes down to one rule: build each rung before the next.

Rung 1 — How do you get your body asleep and your mind awake?

Train these trance techniques, from simplest to finest, until you can reach the “body asleep / mind awake” state at will:

  • Deep descent: very long, progressive relaxation (extended yoga nidra type) to total heaviness — the long version of chapter 10's trance.
  • Fractional induction (inherited from Ericksonian hypnosis): descend, come back halfway, descend deeper — each cycle deepens the trance, as you drive a stake in with small taps.
  • Progressive visualization: build a scene of ever-increasing detail to absorb all the mind into imagery.
  • The mental screen: observe the dark space behind the eyelids until hypnagogic images appear on their own — then watch them without grabbing.
  • The subtle senses: in trance, explore one by one the “imagined senses” (touch an imaginary texture, hear an inner sound) — the direct training of the subtle body.
💡 When to move onYou're ready for rung 2 when you regularly reach: complete heaviness, spontaneous images behind the eyes, and time distortion — without falling asleep. That is the doorway to astral projection while sleeping: the body drifts off, the mind stays awake.

Rung 2 — What are micro-detachments?

The principle: rather than trying to “exit” in one block — which freezes most beginners — you learn to detach only one part of the imagined body at a time. In trance, physical body still, you move a “phantom limb”: it's a kinesthetic imagery sensation, surprisingly vivid, that trains like a muscle.

  • The phantom hand: without moving a muscle, “lift” your imagined hand. Feel it lift off, float, touch an imaginary object. The founding exercise.
  • The ceiling magnet: feel a gentle pull drawing the imagined chest upward.
  • The fishing line: a line gently pulls a point of the subtle body (forehead, sternum) forward.
  • The slide: the imagined body slides a few centimeters toward the feet, as on a very soft slide.
  • The phantom sit-up: the imagined torso sits up while the physical body stays lying down.
  • The tip / fall: let yourself “sink” through the mattress, or tip backward.

One session = ONE micro-gesture, repeated with patience. The day a phantom limb becomes as clear as a real one, achieving astral projection with the full techniques becomes almost easy.

Rung 3 — What are the astral projection exit techniques?

They are seven ways of leaving the body in imagination: this is the rung that shows how to have an out-of-body experience. All seven start from the same state (deep trance + possible vibrations) and differ only by the imagined gesture — choose the one that clicks best with your imagination:

What do the vibrations mean?

They signal that you've reached the threshold of separation. These vibrations — buzzing, gentle electric waves, inner acceleration — are the hallmark of the transition state (well documented in accounts, from Monroe to contemporary practitioners). Golden rule: don't react. Neither fear nor excitement — both dissolve the state. Let the vibrations settle, grow on their own, and then trigger your technique, calmly.

After the exit: stabilize then explore

  1. The first meters: aim for nothing grand — move a meter away, observe the room, come back. Like a diver first learning to breathe underwater.
  2. Moving: this is how to astral travel once you're out — intention replaces the legs. Think of the destination, glide there.
  3. Choose destinations, then keep a map of your explorations in the journal: recurring places become landmarks, your inner geography takes shape.
  4. Return: think of your body, your hand, want to move a finger — the return is instantaneous. Then full grounding and immediate notes.
⚠️ Reminders from chapter 10Occasional (1–2 attempts/week), never with a sleep debt, sleep paralysis = normal and passing, and “objective” verifications to be taken with friendly skepticism: the value of these journeys is in the experience and what it teaches you, not in a proof to be established.
