Practical tips et common mistakes

Everything that keeps a practice going in real life

When, where, how?

🕰 When?

  • In the morning on waking: mind still calm, effect on the whole day
  • At a fixed time: attach the session to an existing habit (after coffee, before the shower)
  • Avoid: right after a heavy meal (drowsiness)

📍 Where?

  • A dedicated corner, even tiny: cushion + corner of a room is enough
  • Phone on airplane mode (except for the timer or the app)
  • Total silence isn't required: noises become supports for attention

🧰 Equipment

  • Nothing mandatory. Useful: firm cushion (zafu), bench, blanket
  • Loose clothes, bare feet or socks
  • A gentle timer — so you don't watch the clock

The 7 beginner mistakes (and their solutions)

MistakeSolution
1 · Wanting to “empty the mind”Aim for “notice and return,” never “stop thinking.” Emptiness isn't the goal, presence is.
2 · Starting too long5 minutes held beats 30 minutes endured. Increase by only 2–3 minutes per week.
3 · Practicing “when you think of it”Same time, same place, same trigger: habit does 80% of the work.
4 · Judging every sessionThere are no “good” or “bad” sessions — there are sessions done. A restless session trains as much as a calm one.
5 · Giving up at the first difficult weekMotivation fluctuates, it's expected. Reduce duration to 3 minutes rather than skipping days.
6 · Jumping from technique to techniqueGive 2 weeks to a technique before changing. Depth comes from repetition.
7 · Seeking “extraordinary states”Chills, lights, great peace: pleasant but anecdotal. The real measure of progress is in daily life — patience, distance, presence.

Frequently asked questions

How long before I “feel” results?
Some effects are immediate (relaxation after a cardiac coherence session). Deeper effects — less reactivity, better concentration — typically appear after 3 to 8 weeks of daily practice. Keep a mini-journal: one line per day after the session, to see the path traveled.
Eyes open or closed?
Both are practiced. Closed: easier for inwardness, risk of drowsiness. Half-closed with gaze downward (zen tradition): more alertness. Test and adopt what stabilizes you best.
Guided or silent meditations?
Guided ones are perfect for learning — that's the role of our audio sessions. Progressively, insert silent sessions (with timer): that's where autonomy is built. A good balance: guided to discover a technique, silence to deepen it.
I have pins and needles in my legs / back pain, what do I do?
Tingling: frequent and benign, observe for 3 breaths then move mindfully if needed. Sharp back or knee pain: change position (chair!) — pain is never a required passage.
Can meditation harm me?
For the vast majority, no. But for people going through acute grief, untreated trauma or active psychological disorder, deep exploration practices (Vipassana, long retreats) can bring up difficult material. In that case: short and grounded techniques (breath, walking), and support from a healthcare professional. If you feel unwell during a session, open your eyes, feel your contact with the ground, move.
Can I meditate with music?
For relaxation, yes. For attention training, prefer silence or natural ambient sounds: music “carries” the mind and does part of the work for you. Exception: the discreet soundscapes of sleep meditations.
