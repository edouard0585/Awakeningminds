Lucid dreaming et conscious sleep

Exploring the night: from dream journal to lucid dream

Definitions and why

Lucid dreaming is a dream in which you know you're dreaming — and where you can sometimes steer what happens. Unlike astral travel, it is solidly documented in the lab: trained lucid dreamers can signal their lucidity by agreed eye movements during REM sleep. About one in two people have had at least one spontaneously.

Why seek it? The vastest inner playground there is: flying, dialoguing with dream characters, transforming a recurring nightmare (a well-documented use), rehearsing a skill, exploring creativity. It's also the gentlest gateway to the states of the Altered states chapter: hypnagogia becomes naturally familiar there.

The complete beginner's method

  1. The dream journal — the absolute foundation: every morning, before any other gesture, note what you remember (even “nothing” or a fragment). Within 2 weeks, recall goes from nothing to several dreams per night. Without recall, no lucidity: you can't become lucid in a dream you forget.
  2. Spot your dream signs: reread the journal each week: themes recur (impossible places, missing people, being late, flying…). These are YOUR future lucidity triggers.
  3. Reality tests (10×/day): several times a day, seriously ask “am I dreaming?” and check: reread a text twice (it changes in dreams), look at your hands (distorted in dreams), pinch your nose closed and try to breathe (air passes through in dreams). Done mechanically it's useless — done with sincere doubt, the reflex migrates into your dreams.
  4. The MILD technique at bedtime: as you fall asleep, relive a recent dream and repeat with intention: “next time I dream, I recognize that I'm dreaming.” Visualize yourself becoming lucid at one of your dream signs.
  5. Wake-back-to-bed (WBTB) — the accelerator: wake after 5–6 h of sleep, 15–20 minutes awake (reread your journal), then go back to bed with MILD. The majority of lucid dreams occur in this late-night sleep, rich in REM.
  6. Once lucid, stabilize: excitement wakes! Rub your hands together in the dream, touch the ground, look at details, say “clarity!”. Then explore calmly, one simple intention at a time.
💡 The link with guided sleepSleep meditations and guided dream journeys prepare the ground: they install the habit of consciously crossing the transition into sleep — exactly the skill MILD and astral travel need. Listen to them as training, not just as sleep aids.
⚠️ CautionWBTB fragments sleep: 2–3 nights per week maximum, never during exhaustion. And if the waking/dream boundary becomes confused or anxiety-provoking, take a full break — ordinary sleep is sacred.
