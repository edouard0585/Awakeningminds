Meditating without sitting down

For anyone stillness doesn't suit

Some days sitting is impossible: the body is too restless, the back protests, the mind spins too fast to stay put. Walking meditation exists for exactly those days — and it isn't a fallback: it's a complete practice, taught for centuries in contemplative traditions.

Its advantage is twofold. Movement absorbs restlessness instead of fighting it, and the sensations of the feet on the ground give attention a far more tangible anchor than the breath. Many people who "can't do it sitting" discover meditation by walking.

2

The three phases of a step

Lift… move… place…

Each step breaks into three beats, which you follow inwardly. That's the whole exercise:

  1. Lift — the heel leaves the ground, weight shifts to the other leg.
  2. Move — the foot travels through the air, knee soft.
  3. Place — the foot meets the ground, weight returns, the base reforms.

Walk much more slowly than usual: three or four times slower. Gaze resting three metres ahead, hands wherever you like — behind, in front, alongside. When the mind leaves, return to the phase you're in, exactly as you'd return to the breath.

💡 The routeTen to fifteen steps in a straight line, then turn around and walk it back. A hallway, a room, a garden is enough — you aren't going anywhere, and a short there-and-back keeps you from thinking about it.
3

Three ways to practise it

From most formal to most discreet
  • The slow version — 10 to 20 minutes on a short route, at the pace described above. The closest to seated meditation.
  • Mindful walking — normal pace, outdoors: you notice the footfalls, the air on your skin, the sounds, without naming the phases. Ideal for the morning commute.
  • The emergency version — three minutes in a corridor when tension rises: the body discharges through movement while attention re-centres.
⚠️ Outdoors, keep your eyes openIt sounds obvious, but the pull toward half-closed eyelids is real. Outside, safety comes before depth: a familiar route, no roads to cross.
