Getting the most from a guided or interactive meditation

How to respond to the guide, explore, and capitalize on every session

Preparing the session

  • Headphones or earbuds: the voice “in your head” carries immersion much better than a speaker.
  • Choose the voice that truly relaxes you (soft, warm, low…): it's the one that will accompany you deeply — this choice matters more than it seems.
  • Choose the setting according to the work you want (see the archetypal places in chapter 14): the same meditation in a forest, cave or cosmos doesn't work the same.
  • Don't be disturbed: airplane mode or focus, 30–45 minutes ahead, lying or sitting depending on the practice (lying for astral and sleep, sitting for the rest).
  • An intention in one sentence before starting: “tonight, I discover,” “I seek clarity on X.” Intention is the rudder of the journey.

The art of answering the guide — the heart of interactive meditations

In an interactive meditation, the guide asks you questions (“what do you notice?”, “where are you?”, “who is there?”) and builds the journey from YOUR answers. Here's how to get the most from it:

  1. Say the first thing that comes. That's THE rule. Don't seek a “good” answer, don't compose: the first image, even banal or blurry, is the right door. The mind that sorts is exactly what we want to bypass.
  2. “Nothing” is a real answer. “I see black,” “I feel nothing”: perfect, say it. The guide knows how to work from the darkness — it has texture, depth, sometimes a point of light. Nothing is a beginning, not a failure.
  3. Describe in the present, in sensations: “I am in a dark room, it's cool, there's a door to the right” rather than “I think I'm imagining some kind of room.” The present installs; the conditional keeps at a distance.
  4. Let yourself be surprised: if a detail appears that you didn't “decide” (an object, a presence, a change of light), signal it — that is precisely the most precious material, the one coming from further than your will.
  5. Explore what attracts you: when the guide offers directions, follow the spontaneous pull, not logic. The object that “calls,” the intriguing door — attraction is the compass of the inner journey.
  6. You can always say no: a door you don't want to open, a presence you'd rather observe from afar — tell the guide. The pace belongs to you, at every moment.

Capitalizing: sanctuary, objects, guides and journal

Each journey produces precious material — good explorers keep it instead of letting it evaporate:

  • The inner sanctuary: when a meditation place truly does you good, adopt it: return there at the start of the session, enrich it. A sanctuary visited ten times becomes accessible in a few breaths — it's your rear base for all explorations.
  • Symbolic objects received: the key, the stone, the book offered during a journey — set them in your sanctuary and write them down. They often resurface several sessions later, with a use: it's your inner inventory.
  • Guides met: when a benevolent figure returns, remember their reconnection sign (chapter 10). A “recurring” guide becomes a reliable shortcut to your deep wisdom.
  • The journal, always: 3 lines minimum after EACH session — place, encounter, symbol, message, feeling. It's what turns a collection of experiences into a path.
💡 If overwhelmed during a sessionIf an emotion or image becomes too intense: open your eyes, name 3 objects in the room, feel your contact with the ground, breathe in 4-6 — grounding first, always. You can resume later, or not: interrupting a session is never a failure, it's practitioner maturity. An emergency grounding button is there for this during sessions.
