What is a body scan?

Feeling, without changing anything

The body scan is the simplest of the great meditation techniques: you walk attention slowly through the body, region after region, and feel what is there — warmth, contact, tingling, heaviness, or nothing at all. That's it. You don't try to relax: relaxation arrives as a by-product, precisely because you're not chasing it.

It's the ideal entry door for people who "think too much": attention has a concrete route to follow, the mind has less room to drift — and when it drifts, the body is a simple place to come back to.

2

The route, step by step

Feet to head, unhurried
  1. Settle in — seated or lying down (lying down if the goal is sleep). Three full breaths to set the scene.
  2. Start with the feet. Toes, soles, heels: what do you feel, right now? The touch of socks, a coolness, nothing? "Nothing" is a valid observation.
  3. Move up slowly: ankles, calves, knees, thighs… then hips, belly, chest — noticing the breath on the way —, back, shoulders.
  4. The talkative regions. Jaw, forehead, neck, shoulders: that's where the day usually lodges. Force nothing — resting attention there is enough; the release comes on its own.
  5. End with the whole body, felt as one piece, for two or three breaths. Then open your eyes — or let sleep finish the job.
💡 When you feel nothingCompletely normal, especially at first: some regions are silent. Move on without judging — sensitivity sharpens session after session; that is exactly the muscle the scan trains.
3

Three uses of the body scan

One tool, three doors
  • Deep relaxation — end of day, 10 to 20 minutes lying down: the body settles layer by layer, like sand sinking through water.
  • Falling asleep — the scan is the heart of evening meditations: started in bed, it rarely finishes before sleep does.
  • Everyday tension — a five-minute seated express version, to spot where the day has clenched and let the shoulders come down.
