1You’re Not Bad at Meditation: You Might Just Be Tired

You settle in.

A voice says:

“Bring your attention to your breath…”

Then:

nothing.

You wake up twenty-seven minutes later with a cushion mark on your cheek.

Welcome to one of the great classics of meditation.

Falling asleep while meditating is extremely common.

It doesn’t mean you’re incapable of meditating.

The first explanation is sometimes much simpler:

you’re short on sleep.

As long as your brain is receiving notifications, light, movement and problems to solve, it stays switched on.

Then you close your eyes.

You stop moving.

The room is quiet.

And your body replies:

“Finally!”

2Why Does Meditation Sometimes Make It Easier to Fall Asleep?

Relaxation and falling asleep share several ingredients:

  • stillness;
  • reduced stimulation;
  • closed eyes;
  • slower breathing;
  • muscle relaxation;
  • repetitive attention;
  • a quiet environment.

Add a slow voice and a thirty-minute body scan in a warm bed, and you’ve essentially sent sleep a handwritten invitation.

Some mindfulness interventions are in fact studied precisely for their ability to improve sleep-related symptoms. (PubMed)

So it helps to tell two goals apart:

  • meditating to train attention;
  • meditating to fall asleep.

In the second case, sleeping isn’t a problem.

In the first, it’s better to change the conditions.

3Seven Simple Ways to Stay Awake

1. Sit Up

The first fix is almost comically effective.

Don’t meditate lying down.

Sit on a chair or a cushion, with your back fairly straight.

Your brain associates this posture with sleep far less.

2. Open Your Eyes Slightly

Meditation absolutely does not require closed eyes.

Rest your gaze softly on a point in front of you, or let it drop toward the floor.

3. Change the Time

If you always meditate at 11:45 p.m. after a twelve-hour day, you don’t need Sherlock Holmes to identify the problem.

Try the morning or the middle of the day.

4. Use a More Active Meditation

Instead of a body scan, choose:

  • attention to sounds;
  • mindful walking;
  • counting breaths;
  • visual observation;
  • standing meditation.

5. Cool the Room Down

A warm, dark, silent room makes an excellent bedroom.

Which is exactly the problem.

A little fresh air can increase alertness.

6. Shorten the Session

Ten truly aware minutes are better than thirty minutes, twenty-two of which are spent snoring.

7. Get More Sleep

Revolutionary technique.

If drowsiness follows you all day long, the solution may not be a more sophisticated meditation posture.

It may be sleep.

4What If Monotony Is to Blame?

Focusing on the breath can feel extraordinarily repetitive.

Breathe in.

Breathe out.

Breathe in.

Breathe out.

Your mind is used to TikTok, messages, music, video, work and multitasking.

Suddenly switching to:

“left nostril slightly cool”

is a fairly spectacular drop in stimulation.

At first, the brain sometimes reads this low stimulation as a signal to sleep.

With practice, you learn to develop calm but alert attention.

In contemplative traditions, alertness is in fact an integral part of the training.

Relaxation is not the same thing as torpor.

The ideal looks like this:

  • a fairly relaxed body;
  • a clear mind;
  • stable attention.

5When Falling Asleep Is Exactly the Right Outcome

Not every session has the same goal.

A sleep meditation, yoga nidra, muscle relaxation or a body scan done in bed can be used specifically to ease the transition into sleep.

In that context, waking up the next morning without having heard the end of the recording is probably an excellent five-star review.

On the other hand, if you’re looking to develop:

  • concentration;
  • observation;
  • emotional awareness;
  • sustained attention;
  • then it’s better to preserve alertness.

So the question to ask isn’t:

“Am I allowed to fall asleep?”

But:

“What was the purpose of this session?”

6When Drowsiness Deserves More Attention

Occasionally falling asleep during meditation is nothing to worry about.

However, if you constantly fall asleep as soon as you’re still, despite nights that seem long enough, or if you experience excessive daytime sleepiness, it may be worth talking to a health professional.

Sleep disorders, certain medications or other factors can cause significant fatigue.

Meditation may then reveal the drowsiness.

It isn’t necessarily the cause.
