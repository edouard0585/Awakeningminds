1Seeing a color around someone: mystical experience, visual effect or intuitive information?

The human aura is one of the most appealing ideas in modern esoteric traditions: every person is said to be surrounded by a subtle field, sometimes described as made up of several layers and different colors reflecting their vitality, temperament, emotions or spiritual state.

A red aura would be dynamic.

A green aura would suggest healing or compassion.

A blue aura, calm and communication.

A violet aura, spirituality.

A yellow aura, intellect or joy.

These correspondences are now everywhere.

The problem is that they often give the impression that an official, universal dictionary of aura colors has existed since antiquity.

It hasn’t.

Modern systems of auras, subtle bodies, colored chakras and energy layers are the result of a historical blend of religious traditions, theosophy, Western occultism, modernized yoga and New Age movements.

Of course, depictions of halos, glories and light around saints, deities or sacred figures have existed for a very long time in many cultures.

But that doesn’t automatically mean all those artists were describing the same “human energy field” in the modern sense.

So the aura is an excellent subject for applying a particularly healthy rule:

respect the experience without turning the interpretation into a fact too quickly.

2Why do some people genuinely say they see an aura?

Some people describe an outline, a light or a color around someone in great detail.

It would be too easy to reply:

“They’re making it up.”

A visual experience can be perfectly genuine while having a different origin from the one imagined.

The human visual system doesn’t work like a camera.

It constantly interprets contrast, movement, brightness, depth and color.

Stare for a long time at a face or a silhouette in front of a light-colored wall.

The outline may seem to shimmer.

A bright zone may appear.

A complementary color can sometimes become visible when your gaze shifts.

This kind of afterimage comes from the adaptation of photoreceptors and from visual processing.

Peripheral vision also contributes to impressions that are less stable than those of central vision.

And if someone has explicitly told you:

“Look carefully around their body to see their aura,”

your attention is now primed to detect the slightest change.

That doesn’t mean every aura experience is necessarily an afterimage.

It simply means the visual system already offers several mechanisms capable of producing phenomena that resemble those described.

3The fascinating case of auras and synesthesia

A much more interesting lead comes from synesthesia, a phenomenon in which one type of stimulus automatically triggers an additional sensory or perceptual experience.

Some people, for example, associate letters with colors.

For others, faces or people can trigger a particular colored experience.

A study published in Consciousness and Cognition looked specifically at the similarities between certain mystical descriptions of auras and the experiences of a synesthete for whom familiar faces triggered a colored mental representation around the silhouette.

It’s fascinating because it shows that a person can genuinely perceive something that closely resembles a colored aura without the hypothesis of an external energy field being necessary.

The subjective experience is real.

The explanation changes.

And this distinction comes up everywhere in the study of unusual states of consciousness.

4Has science detected a human energy field?

The human body obviously generates measurable electrical and magnetic phenomena.

The heart has electrical activity, measured by electrocardiogram.

The brain produces electrical activity, measured notably by EEG.

So do the muscles.

So there really is electrophysiological activity around the body.

But using the word field in this scientific context doesn’t allow us to automatically conclude that the colored auras described in esotericism correspond to these phenomena.

It’s a common logical leap:

  • The body produces electricity.
  • So electromagnetic fields exist.
  • So the spiritual aura is proven.

No.

Between step 2 and step 3, the proof is precisely what’s missing.

To show that a particular energy field corresponds to the aura described by practitioners, you would need to be able to measure it reproducibly and show that it has the claimed characteristics.

A famous test published in JAMA in 1998 examined exactly this ability in practitioners of “Therapeutic Touch,” who claimed they could perceive a human energy field with their hands. Under conditions where they couldn’t see which of the experimenter’s hands was above their own, the practitioners got about 44% of answers right over 280 trials, a result consistent with chance rather than with reliable detection of the claimed field.

This experiment obviously doesn’t address every conceivable claim about auras.

It addresses one specific claim: under these conditions, these practitioners did not demonstrate that they could reliably detect the field they said they perceived.

That’s exactly how a good scientific approach works: a specific question, a specific test, a conclusion limited to what was tested.

5What about Kirlian photography?

You can’t talk about auras without sooner or later coming across a photograph showing an object surrounded by a strange colored glow, with the caption:

“Scientific proof of the human aura.”

Kirlian photography, developed in the 20th century, uses a high-voltage electrical discharge around an object placed on a photographic plate or a similar device.

The beautiful glowing coronas it produces depend on many physical factors, including humidity, pressure, contact, conductivity and electrical settings.

They are impressive.

But they don’t demonstrate the existence of a spiritual aura.

A leaf can produce a Kirlian effect.

So can a coin.

Your spoon has probably not reached enlightenment.

It’s an excellent example of a real phenomenon that can be given a far more spectacular interpretation than what it actually demonstrates.

6A much more interesting middle ground: we are constantly reading other people

Now suppose someone tells you:

“I can feel a dark energy around him.”

There’s a possibility we often forget because it’s less spectacular than an invisible aura, but psychologically far more interesting.

This person may have unconsciously picked up a huge number of signals:

  • posture;
  • facial expression;
  • gaze;
  • physical distance;
  • intonation;
  • speed of speech;
  • micro-hesitations;
  • muscle tension;
  • smell;
  • way of moving.

The brain is continuously processing this social information.

The result can surface consciously as an overall impression:

“Something about him makes me uneasy.”

In energy language:

“His aura is heavy.”

In psychological language:

“My perceptual system has detected several cues I haven’t yet consciously identified.”

Both phrasings can sometimes describe the same lived experience at different levels.

This may be the most fruitful middle ground: some “aura readings” could reflect an extremely fast intuitive reading of body language and emotional state, then translated symbolically into colors, density or energy.

That doesn’t prove all readings work this way.

But it’s a far more testable hypothesis.

7What do aura colors mean in contemporary systems?

The correspondences vary between schools, but you often find:

Red: vitality, action, instinct, grounding.

Orange: creativity, desire, sociability.

Yellow: intellect, confidence, mental activity.

Green: compassion, healing, balance.

Blue: communication, calm, expression.

Indigo: intuition, inner perception.

Violet: spirituality, transformation.

White: purity, integration or a strong spiritual dimension.

These meanings closely resemble the colors attributed today to the seven chakras.

Just remember that these are symbolic systems, not a universal code proven by experiment.

If someone sees blue around you today, there’s no guarantee another aura reader will interpret the same perception in exactly the same way.

So the aura works much better as a symbolic or introspective language than as a diagnostic test.

8An exercise to explore the aura without immediately telling yourself a story

Place a person, or your own hand, in front of a white or light gray background.

Look softly at the outline without staring intensely.

After thirty seconds, notice:

  • the brightness;
  • the contrasts;
  • any changes in color;
  • blurred outlines.

Then shift your gaze to the wall.

Observe any afterimage.

Repeat the exercise under different lighting.

Why?

Because first you learn to know your own visual system.

Then, if you practice a more intuitive approach, look at a person normally and ask yourself:

“What bodily impression arises in me?”

Warmth?

Tension?

Comfort?

Wariness?

Calm?

Then look for the visible cues that might contribute to this impression.

The exercise then becomes much more interesting than a simple guessing game of:

“Green or violet?”

It develops observation, intuition and critical thinking all at once.

That trio is hard to beat.

9So, does the aura exist?

As a spiritual and symbolic representation of the body, of course: the aura has a real cultural history and an important place in many contemporary practices.

As a perceptual experience, some people genuinely see or feel phenomena they describe as auras; synesthesia, visual effects, attention and the intuitive reading of social signals are several possible explanations.

On the other hand, the existence of a colored energy field around the body that matches esoteric descriptions and can be reliably detected is not established by the controlled experiments available. In particular, the classic Therapeutic Touch test did not show any reliable ability among practitioners to detect the field they claimed.

That still leaves an enormous amount of interesting things to explore.

Human perception is strange.

Intuition is fascinating.

The brain is continuously constructing our experience.

So we don’t have to choose between wonder and critical thinking.

We can keep both.
