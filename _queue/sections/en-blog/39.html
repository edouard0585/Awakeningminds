1Meditating on the chakras: a symbolic map for traveling through the whole body

Chakra meditation is probably one of the best-known practices in contemporary spirituality: you place your attention on seven areas of the body in turn, usually from the base of the pelvis up to the top of the head, pairing each one with a color, a psychological quality, a breath and sometimes a mantra.

The most widespread modern version follows this sequence:

  • root chakra — red;
  • sacral chakra — orange;
  • solar plexus — yellow;
  • heart chakra — green;
  • throat chakra — blue;
  • third eye — indigo;
  • crown chakra — violet or white.

This map is useful for meditating, but it’s worth remembering that it’s a modern synthesis of traditions that are far more complex and varied.

Historical tantric texts don’t all describe exactly the same number of chakras, the same colors or the same correspondences.

That’s no problem at all for your practice, as long as you know what you’re using: a contemplative, symbolic map, not an anatomy atlas.

The benefit of this meditation is very concrete.

For twenty minutes, you methodically move through different regions of the body.

You combine:

  • attention;
  • breathing;
  • visualization;
  • interoception;
  • emotion;
  • symbolism.

A recent meta-analysis of 29 randomized trials of mindfulness interventions found a small-to-moderate improvement in self-reported interoceptive awareness, which supports the general idea that training attention on the body can change the way people perceive their internal sensations. (PubMed)

In other words, even if chakras aren’t organs recognized by medicine, systematically paying attention to the body really does train your bodily attention.

2Before going up: start with the ground

Sit down comfortably.

You can settle on a chair, a cushion or even lie down if you’re not likely to fall asleep.

Close your eyes.

For one minute, don’t visualize any chakra.

Simply feel:

  • the points of contact;
  • your weight;
  • your legs;
  • your feet;
  • your breathing.

This step is deliberately plain.

In energy meditations, people are sometimes in such a hurry to “rise” toward the higher centers that they forget the body that serves as the starting point.

So we’re going to do the opposite.

Before the violet light, the great intuitions and cosmic unity:

your feet.

They’re less glamorous.

They’re remarkably effective.

3Root chakra — Muladhara: stability

Place your attention at the base of your pelvis.

Imagine a deep red color.

As you breathe in, feel the volume of your pelvis.

As you breathe out, imagine your weight sinking toward the ground.

Stay here for about two minutes.

Don’t try to “open” anything.

Just feel the stability.

You can silently repeat:

“I am here.”

Muladhara’s theme is grounding.

The body.

Safety.

The ground.

If your attention wanders off into a thought, come back to the feeling of weight.

4Sacral chakra — Svadhisthana: movement and flow

Move your attention a few inches below your navel.

Imagine the color orange.

Observe the natural movement of your belly as you breathe.

You may feel your pelvis relax.

In modern systems, this chakra is traditionally associated with pleasure, creativity, emotion and flow.

Simply ask yourself:

“Where could I bring a little more flexibility today?”

No need to get an answer.

Let the question be there.

Then move on.

5Solar plexus — Manipura: will and the ability to act

Now place your attention in the upper part of your abdomen.

Imagine a warm yellow.

Breathe.

Feel the area move slightly.

The solar plexus is symbolically associated with action, willpower, personal direction and confidence.

You can repeat:

“I can act on what depends on me.”

This wording is deliberately realistic.

Not:

“I control everything that happens to me.”

Because no.

And it’s very restful to remember that.

6Heart chakra — Anahata: openness with boundaries

Place your attention in the middle of your chest.

Imagine the color green.

Feel your rib cage open slightly as you breathe in.

Then relax as you breathe out.

Think of a person, an animal or a place that naturally brings up a little emotional warmth.

Don’t try to produce universal love for all of humanity in thirty seconds.

Start small.

Then repeat:

“Can I stay open without losing myself?”

It’s a beautiful definition of a balanced heart.

7Throat chakra — Vishuddha: expression

Move your attention to your throat.

Imagine a light blue.

Relax your jaw.

You may feel the air passing through.

You can swallow naturally.

The throat chakra is associated with speech, expression and authenticity.

Ask yourself a simple question:

“Is there something I’m not expressing clearly?”

Then let the question go.

Don’t call a family meeting right away.

We’re still meditating.

8Third eye — Ajna: observe before you interpret

Gently place your attention between your eyebrows.

Imagine a point of indigo or deep blue.

Keep your forehead muscles relaxed.

Don’t force your gaze upward.

For two minutes, simply observe the thoughts that appear.

An image.

A sentence.

A sensation.

Then let it go.

Here, third eye work can be understood as training in discernment:

seeing what appears without immediately turning it into absolute truth.

An intuition can be interesting.

It’s still an intuition.

That’s already a lot.

9Crown chakra — Sahasrara: opening up space

Now bring your attention to the top of your head.

You can imagine a white or violet light, or simply a very clear space.

Instead of focusing your attention even more, do the opposite.

Widen it.

Include:

  • sounds;
  • your breathing;
  • your whole body;
  • the space around you.

Stay like this for one to two minutes.

The crown chakra is symbolically associated with transcendence, unity and consciousness.

No need to look for an extraordinary experience.

The feeling of sitting quietly while hearing the room around you is already an experience of consciousness.

And it’s available without a subscription.

10Why you need to come back down after going up

Here’s the step that many chakra meditations forget.

We’ve traveled through the whole body up to the top.

Now let’s come back down.

Third eye.

Throat.

Heart.

Belly.

Pelvis.

Legs.

Feet.

Then stay for one full minute with nothing but the feeling of your feet, or of your body’s contact with the ground.

Why?

Because a good energy practice shouldn’t just intensify sensations or give you the impression of “rising.”

It should also develop your ability to come back to an ordinary, stable, functional state.

So the top isn’t the end.

The root is.

It’s a bit less spectacular.

But much more balanced.

11The full 20-minute session

Minutes 1–2: body and breathing.

Minutes 3–4: root chakra.

Minutes 5–6: sacral chakra.

Minutes 7–8: solar plexus.

Minutes 9–10: heart chakra.

Minutes 11–12: throat.

Minutes 13–14: third eye.

Minutes 15–16: crown chakra.

Minutes 17–19: gradual descent.

Minute 20: feet, natural breathing and surroundings.

You can practice this seven chakra meditation once or twice a week and, on other days, use a meditation focused on a single chakra.

Your progress doesn’t need to be spectacular.

The best indicator isn’t how many colors you see.

It may simply be your ability to feel your body, your emotions and your inner state more clearly.
